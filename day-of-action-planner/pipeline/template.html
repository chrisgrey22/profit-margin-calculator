Developer notes

The problem

Several teams in the Strategic Public Protection Command visit offenders' home addresses, and each one plans its own visits. MOSOVO visits registered sex offenders. The Online Investigation Team checks devices under Sexual Harm Prevention Orders. The Domestic Abuse Unit gives Clare's Law disclosures and checks protection orders. Integrated Offender Management visits its cohort. BCU officers check bail addresses. Planning in silos sends separate crews down the same streets, sometimes to the same front door, on the same day.

This tool brings all of that demand into one plan. It pairs officers from different teams into crews of two and routes each crew so they work together. The specialist always leads their own team's visit, and one trip covers every team that needs the same address.

This page is a working mock-up. Routing, allocation and directions run fully in the browser against a real road network. Only the case data is invented. Use this page as the brief for a production build.

How the planner works

  1. Travel times. Dijkstra's shortest-path search runs from every station and every visit over the road graph. Speeds are set by road class: motorway 100 km/h, trunk 80, A road 56, B road 48, minor 42, unclassified 35, residential 28. There is a 4-second penalty at each urban junction.
  2. Crews. Officers are always paired into crews of two. A supervisor's own pairings come first. Otherwise each MOSOVO or Domestic Abuse Unit officer is paired with a BCU officer from the same station, then specialists with each other, then BCU officers together. Anyone without a partner gets no visits and is flagged.
  3. Eligibility. A crew can take an offender visit only if one member is MOSOVO, and that officer is the named lead. Specialist-only visits need both members to be MOSOVO. Clare's Law disclosures need a Domestic Abuse Unit member. Any crew can take a bail check, led by its BCU officer. A visit must be within the chosen drive time of the crew's base.
  4. Time on site, from arrival, averages 1 hour for an offender visit, 10 minutes for a bail check and 30 minutes for a disclosure. A supervisor can change these on the day.
  5. Allocation. Visits are sorted by priority: overdue, then risk, then specialist-only, then time window. Each one goes to the officer and position that adds the least time while still fitting the shift, the write-up time and any time window.
  6. Improvement. Each route is improved with 2-opt and relocate moves, then any visits left over are tried again.
  7. Directions. Each leg's path is turned into plain-English steps from the road names and the angle of each turn.

Rules to keep in production

  • Every visit is attended by two officers. No lone visits.
  • The named lead on every registered sex offender visit is the MOSOVO officer. A BCU partner never leads outside their own discipline.
  • Specialist-only visits stay with two officers from the owning team.
  • Disclosures respect the times the applicant has said they can safely be seen, and are never planned when the subject is likely to be present.
  • Store as little as possible on devices. Show addresses only to the allocated crew, and log every view.
